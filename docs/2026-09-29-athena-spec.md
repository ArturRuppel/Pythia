# Athena — product specification

Date: 2026-09-29  
Status: initial specification; product requirements agreed in conversation, implementation choices proposed below  
Relationship: writing companion to Pythia

## 1. Purpose

Athena is a workspace for writing scientific papers and proposals by dictation while looking at their argument structure. The human authors the prose. An AI agent records the dictated transcript verbatim, connects passages to structured ideas, and makes separate suggestions.

The primary working arrangement is an iPad displaying both the structure and the prose, with an iPhone providing voice-to-speech through an AI agent. The author speaks freely while consulting the structure. Athena makes the relationship between what the author intended to say and what they have actually said visible through matching colors and linked selections.

Pythia remains the literature library and evidence graph. Athena owns documents in progress and their authoring workflow. The two applications exchange references without requiring the same interface or release cycle.

## 2. Confirmed requirements and proposed defaults

The following requirements come directly from the author:

- The application is named Athena.
- Begin with a tokenized representation of a paper or proposal, building on the existing Pythia proposal workflow.
- Make the structure editable by humans and agents.
- Provide a full-text editor alongside the structure; both views can remain open simultaneously.
- Optimize the workflow for an iPad paired with iPhone voice-to-speech through an AI agent.
- The human dictates prose freely while looking at the structure.
- Capture dictated text verbatim. The agent may suggest changes but must not silently rewrite the prose.
- The agent's principal editing task is to connect prose and structure using color codes.
- Keep Athena distinct from Pythia while allowing them to interact.

Specific layouts, storage formats, synchronization mechanisms, and suggestion controls in this document are proposed implementation defaults. They are not decisions already made by the author. This specification authorizes no migration of existing documents or deployment of a service.

## 3. Core objects

### Document and structure

A document contains a title, sections, an ordered argument structure, prose, links, suggestions, and revision history. Partial documents are valid.

A **unit** is a meaningful piece of the argument, not an LLM token or an individual word. Examples include a claim, question, hypothesis, planned test, or method. Units have stable identities independent of wording and position. Their text, kind, ordering, relationships, and evidence references are editable without requiring YAML editing.

The existing Pythia slice and programme models provide the initial vocabulary. Athena should preserve imported kinds and references rather than inventing an incompatible scientific ontology. A structural section is an organizational container, not automatically a scientific claim. An idea can be reused in multiple parts of a document without duplicating its identity.

### Prose

Prose is independently authored, continuous text. It is not generated by concatenating unit labels. Paragraphs can contain several ideas, transitions, repetitions, incomplete thoughts, or ideas not yet represented in the structure.

The editor supports ordinary direct human editing as well as insertion from the agent. Human edits do not need AI approval. Initial formatting needs are headings, paragraphs, emphasis, lists, and citation references; publication layout is a later concern.

### Connections

A connection links a prose span to one or more structural units. Multiple spans can express the same unit. Connections record interpretation; they do not establish that the prose is scientifically correct or that a source supports every word in it.

Three relationships must remain distinguishable:

1. A passage **expresses** an argument unit.
2. An argument unit **refers to or is supported/challenged by** Pythia material.
3. A suggestion **proposes a change** to prose, structure, or a connection.

Linking a passage to a literature-backed unit must not silently turn that passage into a verified claim or insert a citation into the prose.

## 4. Primary writing session

1. Open a document on the iPad. The structure and prose are both visible.
2. Choose a section and a prose insertion point. The active dictation destination is clearly labeled and remains stable when inspecting other units or sources.
3. Use the iPhone's agent conversation to dictate. The phone handles speech input; Athena receives the text through the agent integration.
4. The agent inserts the received transcript at the selected destination without changing its wording. New prose appears on the iPad without a reload.
5. At the author's request, or at an agreed pause in dictation, the agent connects the new passage to relevant units. These links can be added without changing prose.
6. Selecting either a passage or a unit reveals the corresponding material in the other pane using matching colors and labels.
7. Continue dictating, rearrange the argument, edit directly, or open suggestions when useful. Unlinked prose and unwritten units remain valid working states.

The author may ask the agent to link a completed section rather than link after every utterance. Linking must not block capture or interrupt the author's train of thought.

## 5. Verbatim capture and author control

**Verbatim means the text received from voice-to-speech.** Athena cannot promise fidelity to the original audio when only a transcript is available. It must preserve that transcript exactly, including repetitions, unusual wording, punctuation supplied upstream, and apparent recognition errors. Store the original input separately from later document revisions.

The agent must not silently correct grammar, remove filler, improve style, infer missing words, strengthen claims, insert citations, or complete an unfinished thought. Suspected transcription errors appear as suggestions. Direct instructions such as “replace this word with that word” authorize that specific edit; general requests to review a passage do not authorize a rewrite.

The workflow must distinguish **dictation** from **instructions to the agent**. Proposed default: an explicit dictation mode or a clearly delimited dictation message, with a visible destination. Do not infer commands from arbitrary words inside dictated prose. Ambiguous mixed messages are retained without executing uncertain edits; ask which part belongs in the document.

Suggestions are separate records containing the target, proposed change, and reason. Accepting one applies only that change; rejecting one leaves the document untouched. Suggestions based on an older revision must be rechecked before applying. Nothing is accepted merely because the author continues dictating.

Agent edits to the structure are allowed when requested. Mapping a new idea found in prose to a newly invented unit is a structural change, not routine linking: propose the addition unless the author has authorized structure expansion. Agent-created connections can be applied as reversible metadata and remain visibly attributable to the agent.

## 6. iPad workspace

### Simultaneous views

The primary layout is a resizable split view: structured outline/cards on one side and continuous prose on the other. Neither pane is just a preview. Both support editing. The structure can expose dependencies and evidence on demand; a free-form graph canvas is not required for the first version.

Landscape uses side-by-side panes. Portrait offers a stacked split with both views still available together. A temporary focused view may be offered, but it must preserve a quick return to the two-pane workspace and both reading positions.

Touch controls should be comfortably usable on an iPad without hover or a hardware keyboard. Reordering has both drag and explicit move controls. Opening the keyboard, selecting text, rotating the device, or opening a suggestion must not lose the insertion point or draft.

### Connection display

- Give units a stable display color within the document and a short visible label.
- Use subtle margin marks or underlines by default to preserve comfortable reading.
- Selecting a unit strengthens its highlights in the prose; selecting a passage emphasizes its linked units.
- For overlapping connections, show multiple markers or a compact list rather than blending colors into an ambiguous background.
- Provide “next linked passage” and explicit reveal controls for distant matches. Avoid forcing both panes to jump on every touch.
- Color is supplemented by labels, outlines, and selection states; relationships must be understandable without color discrimination.
- With many units, reuse a limited palette with distinct labels. Do not assume a unique distinguishable hue exists for every idea in a long document.

Unlinked prose receives a neutral indicator when the author requests coverage inspection. Units with no prose show an unwritten state. Neither is an error. Coverage measures connection completeness, not scientific quality or readiness for submission.

### Suggestions and evidence

Suggestions open in a drawer or local panel, leaving the working context recoverable. They do not replace the authored text with a proposed version. Evidence links open the corresponding Pythia unit or source passage and provide a clear route back to Athena.

## 7. Connection lifecycle during editing

Connections must survive edits when their identity remains unambiguous and become visibly unresolved when it does not.

- Inserting text before a linked passage moves its anchor without changing the linked content.
- Moving a paragraph within the document carries its connections.
- Rewriting a linked span retains its previous association as needing review; it does not certify that the new wording still expresses the same claim.
- Deleting the entire linked span retires its active connection and preserves the event in history.
- Splitting or merging paragraphs preserves traceable spans where possible and flags ambiguous boundaries.
- Splitting or merging structural units requires explicit reassignment or review of their existing connections.
- Changing a unit's scientific meaning marks dependent connections for review; simple reordering does not.
- Deleting a unit never deletes the prose linked to it.

The app must never silently reconnect an ambiguous anchor to the first matching phrase elsewhere in the document. Proposed anchor strategy: stable block identity, a revision-aware range, and a stored text excerpt for validation. The implementation may choose another strategy if it satisfies these behaviors.

Semantic mismatch detection is assistance, not a guarantee. For example, a rewrite from “may influence” to “determines” should be available for review without relying on an AI detector to catch every strengthening of a claim.

## 8. Pythia boundary and existing material

Pythia owns published-paper metadata, curated slices, exact source quotes, and literature relationships. Athena owns draft prose, document structure, its uses of literature, draft-specific connections, and authoring history.

Athena stores stable Pythia identifiers with enough display information to show a meaningful reference when Pythia is unavailable. Where available, record the source revision or a snapshot so later evidence changes can be detected. Broken or changed references remain visible; they are never silently dropped.

Reading and referencing Pythia is part of normal writing. Modifying Pythia's curated knowledge is a separate, explicitly authorized curation action. A draft claim or speculative hypothesis does not become a curated literature claim by being written in Athena.

The existing [programme design](2026-08-02-programme-graph-design.md) separates the argument from narrative ordering and excludes graph-generated prose. Athena retains that separation while adding human-authored prose and explicit links. It proposes a separate authoring application rather than changing Pythia's reading workflow in place.

Initial import should accept the existing section/bullet/reference narrative form and relevant programme units. Preserve original identifiers and source references. Imported bullets can seed the structure; do not silently treat them as newly dictated prose. Existing prose may be imported separately with its origin recorded. Import is repeatable without duplicating previously imported objects, and leaves the source files unchanged.

This public specification contains no actual proposal text or private research results. Real document content remains in private storage, separate from reusable application code.

## 9. Agent and synchronization contract

Athena needs a documented integration that an agent can use from the iPhone conversation. It must not depend on the agent controlling the iPad screen or on a specific model vendor. The exact connector, API, and hosting arrangement remain to be selected.

The minimum operations are:

- Read the active document, current revision, structure, and explicit dictation destination.
- Insert verbatim text with the original transcript and a unique operation identifier.
- Read bounded prose spans and their links.
- Add, revise, or remove connections without modifying prose.
- Propose a change separately from applying it.
- Apply an explicitly authorized text or structure edit against its expected revision.
- Report whether an operation was saved, rejected, or is awaiting resolution.

Proposed synchronization requirements:

- The phone's agent and iPad editor act on the same document identity and revision history.
- Retrying a delivered operation does not duplicate dictated text or connections.
- A change to the active document or insertion point cannot redirect an already submitted dictation operation to another destination.
- Concurrent human and agent edits do not silently overwrite each other. Stale operations are safely rebased only when unambiguous; otherwise retain the input and expose the conflict.
- The iPad indicates saved, saving, and disconnected states. An agent reports success only after persistence is acknowledged.
- Text captured locally during a disconnect is retained. A remotely dictated message is only described as delivered after the server acknowledges it; the UI must not imply that an unreachable device received it.
- Reconnection fetches missed revisions and reconciles pending operations without duplication.

Authentication, transport security, and document-scoped access are required for remote writes. Send the agent only material relevant to the requested operation; access to one document must not imply access to the entire private library. Audio retention is not required by Athena's first version.

## 10. Persistence and reversibility

Persist document structure, authored prose, original dictation inputs, connections, suggestions, and revision history. Each operation records its author or agent, target, and revision. A reload or service restart must preserve accepted work.

Human and agent changes are undoable. Undo must reverse the selected operation without erasing unrelated later edits. For complicated conflicts, expose a recoverable prior version rather than pretending a destructive reversal is safe.

Use an inspectable, documented export that includes prose, structure, stable identifiers, and connections. A plain-text or Markdown prose export is also required. The storage engine and editor framework are implementation choices; the essential constraint is that prose and links cannot become irretrievable inside a proprietary editor representation.

## 11. First prototype

Build the smallest complete writing loop around one section:

1. Import or create a small argument outline with stable units and Pythia references.
2. Display editable structure and continuous prose together on an iPad.
3. Connect an iPhone agent session and insert real dictated transcripts verbatim into an explicit destination.
4. Let the agent add many-to-many passage connections and expose them through linked colors and labels.
5. Provide separate suggestions with individual acceptance and rejection.
6. Preserve draft and connection history through ordinary edits, retries, reloads, and disconnections.
7. Export the prose and a complete structured document bundle.

Use synthetic material during development. Test on a private copy of an existing proposal section only when building that prototype is authorized. Do not mutate or migrate the original V2 material as part of this specification.

## 12. Acceptance scenarios

| Scenario | Required result |
|---|---|
| Dictate a transcript with repetition and an apparent recognition error | Stored input and inserted prose match the received transcript exactly; any correction is a separate suggestion. |
| Dictated content contains words that sound like an editing instruction | In dictation mode they remain text and do not execute a command. |
| Retry the same insertion after an acknowledgement is lost | One copy of the text is present. |
| Inspect another unit while dictation is in flight | The text goes to the explicitly selected destination, not the unit most recently inspected. |
| Link one passage to two units, and one unit to two passages | Both directions are discoverable without ambiguous color mixing. |
| Move a linked paragraph | Its connections move with it. |
| Rewrite or delete a linked claim | Connections become reviewable or retired; unrelated text is not substituted. |
| Reorder or remove a structural unit | Prose remains intact; affected links remain understandable. |
| Agent proposes smoother wording | Original prose is unchanged until explicit acceptance. |
| Human edits a passage before accepting an older suggestion | The stale suggestion does not overwrite the human edit. |
| Phone agent and iPad edit concurrently | Both contributions are preserved or a visible, recoverable conflict is raised. |
| Reload or reconnect after a saved operation | Saved content returns; pending retries do not duplicate it. |
| Open on iPad in either orientation | Prose and structure can be displayed together with usable touch controls. |
| View without relying on color | Labels and selection states still expose every connection. |
| Pythia is unavailable or a referenced unit changes | Prose remains usable and the reference's unavailable or changed state is visible. |
| Export a document | Prose is readable independently and the full bundle retains structure, links, and identifiers. |

## 13. Deferred scope and open decisions

The first version does not require autonomous prose generation, native speech recognition, page-perfect typesetting, a complete reference manager, multi-author collaboration, or migration of Pythia's programme graph. Whole-document authoring is an intended use; the first prototype tests the essential interaction on one section.

Resolve these through a prototype rather than treating them as settled requirements:

- Which agent transport provides a reliable iPhone-to-Athena connection, and where does the service run?
- What is the least intrusive way to distinguish dictation from instructions in the chosen agent interface?
- Should automatic linking run after each completed dictation message or only when requested? In either case it must not interrupt capture.
- Which outline/card presentation and split proportions work best on the author's iPad?
- Which editor and storage model preserve robust anchors, exact transcript capture, and readable exports?
- How should Athena-owned structures coexist with existing programme nodes over time?
- Which final document formats are needed after the writing interaction has been validated?

Success means the author can keep looking at the argument, speak in their own words, and inspect how those words connect to it—without the agent taking over the prose.
